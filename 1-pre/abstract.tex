\chapter{Abstract}

Recent progress in generative artificial intelligence (AI) has brought renewed attention to and expanded the possibilities for human-machine co-creativity. However, realising this possibility depends on answering a key question at the interaction layer: how can we design interactions between humans and AI so that their unique forms of creativity are mutually enhanced and blended, while human agency is maintained? Addressing this question is the core aim of my thesis.

Numerous interaction design principles and frameworks exist in the field of human-computer interaction, yet specific principles for human-AI co-creativity remain underdeveloped. This thesis combines an analysis of emerging literature with original interaction design and creative practice-led research to fill this gap.

Co-creativity requires mutual influence and understanding between participants. However, current linear and request-based human-computer interaction paradigms are limited in affording such influence and understanding. I argue that modelling dialogue is a promising approach. In dialogic interaction, participants engage in iterative exchanges, shaping each other's perspectives and co-constructing meaning. This thesis investigates how such dialogic interaction can enable more effective human-AI co-creativity.

I adopt a four-step methodology: (1) defining a framework for dialogic co-creativity, (2) developing case studies by building, using, and testing generative AI systems, (3) analysing these case studies through the lens of the framework, and (4) deriving design principles by synthesising the findings with emerging literature.

As part of a wider research project, I propose a framework for dialogic co-creativity, consisting of a set of actions and principles for designing artificial intelligence that is co-creative with humans through tight creative dialogues. The framework distinguishes between interaction \textit{through} and \textit{about} the creation, and identifies six principles characterising dialogic co-creativity: bidirectional communication, shared collaborative space, mutual influence, mutual understanding, iteration, and context-awareness.

I then use this framework as an analytical lens across three case studies spanning writing, image generation, and new media music. In Chapter 4, I develop a dialogic co-creative writing system integrating a collaborative text editor with a conversational space. Across two user studies, I show that this interface leads to greater user involvement and perceived agency compared to chat-only interfaces. In Chapter 5, I present a case study conducted in collaboration with a nationally printed Australian magazine, exploring the potential and challenges of image-based generative AI in real-world creative workflows. I identify key challenges including limited support for iterative refinement and difficulty communicating precise creative intent, while also observing how training on custom datasets enabled a form of meta-dialogue. In Chapter 6, I describe the creation of two generative public art installations leveraging language models for real-time audiovisual performance, demonstrating the potential for context-aware co-creative systems while revealing the challenges of balancing AI creative agency with human agency.

From this analysis, I derive six design principles for Dialogic Co-Creative AI: Design for Communication, Design for Collaboration, Design for Mutual Influence, Design for Mutual Understanding, Design for Iteration, and Design for Context. Additionally, I propose a meta-principle, Design for Roles and Motivations, as a starting point for prioritising and adapting the other principles to specific users and contexts. Each principle is accompanied by specific guidelines for implementation.

This thesis contributes a theoretical framework for dialogic co-creativity as an interaction design concept and a set of actionable design principles for Dialogic Co-Creative AI. It is my hope that it also inspires artists and practitioners to explore the creative possibilities at the intersection with other intelligences.
