\chapter{New Roles for Generative AI Co-Creators: Two Case Studies in New Media Public Art Installations}

\section{Link to thesis}

\hl{In this final case study chapter, I continue exploring the interaction design space of dialogic human-AI co-creativity, now turning to new media practice and public art installations. This chapter presents two installations in which generative language models autonomously translate environmental data into evolving soundscapes, exploring a novel co-creative role for AI as a semantic interpreter.
The first installation, commissioned for the ANU School of Cybernetics, generated soundscapes in response to weather in Canberra, social media activity, economic indicators, and atmospheric CO2 levels. The second, commissioned for the Sydney Opera House, produced a continually evolving, month-long soundscape driven by data on the building's energy usage, performances, and water consumption. Both were developed in collaboration with music studio Uncanny Valley.
These installations demonstrate the potential of context-aware co-creative systems while highlighting challenges central to dialogic co-creativity. Without a channel for real-time communication with the model, we could not steer its behaviour during performance, limiting our ability to address issues such as generative fixation or to iteratively refine outputs. This reinforces the value of bidirectional communication and shared collaborative spaces identified in previous chapters. Addressing RQ2, the installations also reveal the challenges of balancing AI creative agency with human agency when the system operates autonomously without a direct channel for real-time communication.}
