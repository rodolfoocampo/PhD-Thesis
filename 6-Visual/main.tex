\chapter{Integrating Generative AI into Creative Workflows: Dealing with Consistency, Scene Control and Refinement in a Professional Image Generation Case Study} \label{c:tc6} 

\section{Link to thesis}

\begin{highlight}In the previous chapter, I developed a series of prototypes tested in user studies, primarily focusing on the impact of two dialogic elements: bidirectional communication and a shared collaborative space. In this chapter, I shift the focus to the domain of image-based generative AI through a real-world creative production case study, examining the role of another dialogic co-creativity element: iteration. Rather than examining a system designed with specific dialogic principles, this chapter serves a complementary purpose: by analysing a system that exemplifies \textit{pseudo-dialogue}, I examine how the limited presence of dialogic elements affects the effectiveness of human-AI co-creativity.\end{highlight}

\begin{highlight}As established in Chapter 3, pseudo-dialogue of the sort exemplified in this case study represents the most limited form of dialogic interaction. The interaction is linear: the user provides an instruction (for example, a prompt) and the computer generates. The user can evaluate the output and adjust the next prompt accordingly, but the computer does not iterate on the ongoing creation; it merely generates a completely new output. Moreover, the system does not form a model of the user based on an iterative back-and-forth, nor is there a conversational interface where mutual understanding can happen. The system is simply a generator; it is the user who adjusts their mental model of how the system works based on repeated interaction, and iterates inputs accordingly. Text-to-image systems exemplify pseudo-dialogue, as illustrated in Figure~\ref{fig:levels-dialogue}.\end{highlight}

    \centering

\begin{highlight}In this case study, I engaged with the system from the perspective of practice-based research, rather than through a user study. I collaborated with the Australian Financial Review to produce visuals for their 2023 Power Issue, which features individuals they deem to be the most powerful people in Australia. The aim was to produce "impossible photography" of the subjects to tell stories about their personality and work that would be difficult or impossible with traditional photography. I describe and examine my experience as a creative practitioner working with the creative team of the magazine. A core objective of this case study was to examine the limitations of current tools in supporting real-world creative production, particularly through the lens of the dialogic interaction framework.\end{highlight}

\begin{highlight}To analyse how the absence of dialogic elements affects co-creative processes, I briefly examine the system against each of the six elements of dialogic co-creativity introduced in Chapter 3:\end{highlight}

\begin{itemize}
    \item \begin{highlight}\textbf{Bidirectional Communication:} The system does not engage in bidirectional interaction in the language domain. It merely takes in language input from the user (prompts) and produces an artefact in the image domain. There is no conversational interface, no ability for the system to ask clarifying questions, seek feedback, or engage in dialogue about the creative goals or process.\end{highlight}
    
    \item \begin{highlight}\textbf{A Shared Collaborative Space:} The user and AI do not share a collaborative space where both can make contributions. The system operates purely as a generator: it produces complete images from scratch based on prompts, but cannot modify existing images, make incremental edits, or build upon user-created content. The user cannot directly contribute to the image at the pixel level or make edits that the AI can then respond to.\end{highlight}
    
    \item \begin{highlight}\textbf{Mutual Influence:} Mutual influence refers to the ongoing mutual adaptation that occurs in co-creative dialogue, where both parties influence each other's perspectives. In human-genAI interaction, user-to-AI influence may manifest through the ability to control and steer outputs, while AI-to-user influence may occur when the AI introduces new creative possibilities or directions. In this case study, my ability to influence or control the system resided primarily in prompt engineering and in training the model with specific datasets. Conversely, the system influenced the creative process by enabling new creative possibilities (impossible photography) and occasionally introducing novelty. However, as a text-to-image system operating through pseudo-dialogue, this represented a limited form of mutual influence.\end{highlight}
    
    \item \begin{highlight}\textbf{Mutual Understanding:} Forming mutual understanding is limited. It is the user who models the system's behaviour through repeated interaction, learning how to craft effective prompts, but the AI does not model the human's goals, preferences, or creative intentions. The system has no memory of previous interactions or understanding of the user's evolving creative vision.\end{highlight}
    
    \item \begin{highlight}\textbf{Iteration:} Iteration is severely limited, representing a primary challenge in this case study. Two key elements that enable effective iteration—consistency and refinement—were largely absent. The system cannot maintain consistency across iterations, nor can outputs be incrementally refined or edited, given the model lacks editing capabilities. The user can only iterate by generating entirely new images with adjusted prompts, not by refining promising directions through incremental modifications. Without the ability to maintain consistency or perform incremental refinement, it becomes difficult to explore and evolve promising images, which is a crucial dynamic in creative processes. As detailed in the paper below, these limitations constrained the iterative exploration that characterises effective co-creative dialogue.\end{highlight}
    
    \item \begin{highlight}\textbf{Context-awareness:} The model does not generate with meaningful awareness of the context in which the output is being made. For example, it lacks knowledge of branding elements or aesthetic guidelines for the magazine. It has limited knowledge of the world and limited ability for the user to include goals and preferences in the model. To address this, I trained the model with a dataset of images, using this as a primary steering mechanism to inject some contextual knowledge, but this remains a workaround rather than true context-awareness.\end{highlight}
\end{itemize}

\begin{highlight}The absence of dialogic elements can influence roles and effectiveness within human-AI co-creative processes. The lack of dialogic elements contributed to a role distribution where I, as the user and creative, acted primarily in the \textit{intentional space}, responsible for formulating creative objectives and converting them into prompts, curating outputs and training data, while the AI remained in the \textit{action space}, producing images but not engaging at a conceptual creative level. This case study suggests that the absence of dialogic elements can contribute to a client-producer dynamic rather than true co-creative collaboration. These insights contribute to R1: How to enable co-creative dialogues between humans and AI, and how does it influence the roles and effectiveness of human-AI co-creativity?\end{highlight}

\begin{highlight}The insights gained from this case study also inform R2, exploring design principles for human-AI co-creativity, specifically: the importance of enabling, at a minimum, iterative processes for effective human-AI interaction in creative activities. The paper shows that these iterative processes hinge on the ability to maintain some aspects of the product consistent while refining and editing others.\end{highlight}
